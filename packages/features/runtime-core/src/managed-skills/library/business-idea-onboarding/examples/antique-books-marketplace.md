# Example: Antique Books Marketplace

## Current Read

The idea is a focused marketplace for rare and antique books, starting with very old material such as 15th and 16th century books. The likely first wedge is not "Catawiki but cheaper"; it is a narrower category with stronger expertise, better seller trust, and a clearer buyer experience. The marketplace cold start is the central tension: without credible supply there is no buyer reason to return, but without buyer confidence sellers have little reason to list. The first proof should probably focus on one side of the market, one book category, and a manually curated auction or private-sale workflow before building a broad platform.

## One-Liner

A curated marketplace and auction path for rare antique books.

## Situation

Greenfield

## Current Thesis

A narrower, book-specific marketplace can win trust and liquidity before expanding, especially if it starts with curated supply and lower initial take rates.

## Customer / User / Buyer / Guest

Collectors, specialist dealers, institutions, and high-intent rare book buyers.

## Need / Occasion / Existing Alternatives

Buyers need trusted sourcing and provenance. Sellers need qualified demand and a credible selling process. Existing alternatives include auction houses, dealer networks, Catawiki-style general marketplaces, and private relationships.

## Offer / Positioning

Specialist, lower-friction, book-first marketplace with auctions or curated sale events.

## Contradictions And Tensions

- Lower margins may help recruit sellers, but trust and expertise may matter more than price.
- Building a marketplace too early could distract from the manual liquidity work needed first.

## Next Recommended Move

Define the first supply wedge: which exact book segment, seller type, and first 10 credible listings would make the marketplace worth visiting.

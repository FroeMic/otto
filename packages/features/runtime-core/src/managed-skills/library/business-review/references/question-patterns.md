# Question Patterns

Use one focused question at a time. Good questions change the recommendation.

## Demand

- Who already spends time, money, or political capital on this problem?
- What do they do today instead?
- What makes the current alternative painful enough to switch?
- What recent trigger makes this urgent now?

## Customer

- Who is the first buyer, user, guest, or decision maker?
- Which customer segment would be easiest to reach this month?
- Who has the strongest pain and the shortest path to saying yes?
- Who would be a bad first customer even if they like the idea?

## Offer

- What is the smallest offer someone could buy or try?
- What outcome does the customer get after using it?
- What would make the offer clearly better than the status quo?
- What part of the offer should not exist yet?

## Distribution

- Where can the first ten qualified prospects be found?
- Who already has trust with the target customer?
- What channel could produce evidence fastest?
- What channel looks attractive but is too slow for the current phase?

## Economics

- What does the customer currently pay, directly or indirectly?
- What gross margin or labor constraint could make this hard to operate?
- What must be true for this to become a real business, not a project?
- What price would make the offer feel serious but still easy to test?

## Operations

- What has to happen every time a customer says yes?
- What manual process is acceptable at the start?
- Which operational failure would damage trust?
- What should stay concierge until demand is proven?
